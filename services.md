<p class="lead">The BioHub closes three gaps that have held back bioindustrial manufacturing in Central Massachusetts: shared <strong>pilot-scale capacity</strong>, data-driven <strong>process optimization</strong>, and <strong>workforce training</strong> designed for non-therapeutic biomanufacturing. Screening and contract fermentation are available today through WPI CERES and the WPI Bioprocess Center; the full portfolio comes online as pilot infrastructure is built out over the next three years.</p>

<p class="lead">We work three ways: <strong>fee-for-use</strong> access, <strong>contracts</strong>, and <strong>collaborative research</strong>. Only collaborative research involves WPI IP interests. Get in touch and we'll scope which one fits.</p>

<div class="split" id="scale">
  <div class="split-l">01 — Pilot Facility</div>
  <div class="prose">
    <h2>Scale your process</h2>
    <p>The <strong>BioHub Pilot Facility</strong> is being designed to support both aerobic and anaerobic fermentation with integrated downstream operations. Sensors and automated sampling will log every run, so scale-up rests on data rather than on a repeated batch.</p>
    <p>Downstream is specified for <strong>bioindustrial</strong> products — high volume at lower unit value — which need different unit operations than pharmaceutical purification: evaporation, spray drying, and membrane filtration at scale. Existing fermentation capacity in the region was built around pharmaceutical separations, and that is the gap this facility closes.</p>
    <h3>Unit operations on their own</h3>
    <p>Not every project needs a fermentation campaign. The Pilot Facility is being designed so media formulation, mixing, and downstream steps can be booked independently — for teams that already have material, or already have a strain, and need one operation at volume.</p>
    <details>
      <summary>Planned equipment &amp; specs — Pilot Facility</summary>
      <p>A 10,000 sq ft pilot facility supporting both aerobic and anaerobic fermentation, now in design and permitting. Construction follows approval; the first scale-up projects open once the space is commissioned.</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Bioreactors at 1L, 10L, and 100L — one order of magnitude per step (planned)</li>
        <li>Media formulation and mixing vessels, bookable independently of fermentation (planned)</li>
        <li>Downstream operations: harvest, centrifugation, membrane filtration, evaporation, spray drying (planned)</li>
        <li>Automated sampling, real-time sensor networks, and AI-driven process control (planned)</li>
        <li>Anaerobic-rated workstations and bioreactors for emerging microbiome applications</li>
      </ul>
    </details>

<div class="split" id="engineer">
  <div class="split-l">02 — WPI BioFoundry</div>
  <div class="prose">
    <h2>Engineer your strain</h2>
    <p>The <strong>WPI BioFoundry</strong> is the planned expansion of CERES for high-throughput strain engineering. Automated DBTL (design-build-test-learn) cycles generate the data needed to train AI models for strain and process optimization — letting teams iterate faster on host selection, pathway design, and production conditions. Screening runs from <strong>100 µL to 500 mL</strong>, feeding directly into the Pilot Facility's 1L–100L range.</p>
    <details>
      <summary>Planned equipment &amp; specs — BioFoundry expansion</summary>
      <ul>
        <li>Bench-scale stirred-tank bioreactors to bridge microbioreactor screening and pilot scale (planned)</li>
        <li>Expanded liquid-handling automation for parallel DBTL cycles (planned)</li>
        <li>Microbioreactor scale-down studies linked to pilot-scale data for de-risked scale-up</li>

<div class="split" id="prototype">
  <div class="split-l">03 — MBI Builder's Lab</div>
  <div class="prose">
    <h2>Build the equipment</h2>
    <p>The <strong>MBI Builder's Lab</strong> is a 4,500 sq ft prototyping space operated with MBI, where process-technology startups design, build, and test new bioreactor and unit-operation hardware alongside the people who would run it. Co-location with MBI connects equipment developers to the regional bioindustrial network. Build-out is underway.</p>
    <p>The gap is sharpest downstream. Regional capacity was built for pharmaceutical purification, so a company developing separation, evaporation, or drying equipment for bioindustrial streams has nowhere to test it against real fermentation broth at volume. The Pilot Facility, a short walk away, supplies the broth.</p>
    <details>
      <summary>Planned equipment &amp; specs — Builder's Lab</summary>
      <ul>
        <li>Shared rapid-prototyping equipment (planned)</li>
