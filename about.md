<div class="split">
  <div class="split-l">Where this comes from</div>
  <div class="prose">
    <p>The Blackstone Valley is where American industry learned to work — fifty miles of mills from Worcester to Providence, and a city at the head of the river that built the machines everyone else used. WPI was founded there in 1865 on the idea that theory and practice belong in the same building.</p>
    <p>Biology is now a manufacturing technology, and the same problem has returned: the science is ready and the infrastructure is not. The BioHub is shared pilot-scale capacity at Gateway Park, so a company with a working process can scale it here.</p>
    <p><strong>Same river. Same ethos. New century.</strong></p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="mission">
  <h2>Mission</h2>
  <p class="pillars"><span>Infrastructure</span><span>Innovation</span><span>Workforce</span></p>
  <p>The BioHub gives companies in Central Massachusetts shared access to the equipment, process development, and trained people to run fermentation at pilot scale — so bioindustrial products invented at the bench can be made here, and bioindustrial companies can start and grow here.</p>
</div>

<div class="split">
  <div class="split-l">Vision</div>
  <div class="prose">
    <p>A Central Massachusetts where a bioindustrial company never has to leave to scale.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="split">
  <div class="split-l">Why now</div>
  <div class="prose">
    <p>The U.S. Department of War has named biomanufacturing one of its six <strong>Critical Technology Areas</strong>. McKinsey Global Institute has estimated that biological innovation in materials, chemicals, and energy could generate <strong>$200–300 billion in direct economic impact per year</strong> by 2030–40. The infrastructure that will scale this industry is being built right now — and the regions that build it first will lead it.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="split">
  <div class="split-l">Where we sit</div>
  <div class="prose">
    <p>The BioHub is anchored at <strong>WPI's Gateway Park in Worcester</strong> — the heart of the Commonwealth and one of New England's fastest-growing cities. In 2022 CBRE ranked Worcester <strong>15th in the country</strong> for life-sciences research talent, citing "some of the strongest concentrations of various life sciences researchers in the US." The region's industrial heritage along the Blackstone River — birthplace of the American Industrial Revolution — has given way to a modern innovation economy led by healthcare, education, and the biosciences.</p>
    <p>We build on top of WPI's <strong>Biomanufacturing Education and Training Center (BETC)</strong>, which trained more than <strong>3,000 biomanufacturing professionals</strong> in its first 11 years, and WPI's <strong>Cell Engineering Research Equipment Suite (CERES)</strong>, an established research facility serving a multi-year base of WPI faculty and partner companies.</p>
    <p>MBI's <a href="https://mbi.bio/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Regional-Biomanufacturing-Strategy.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">Regional Biomanufacturing Strategy</a> defined the gaps the BioHub is built to close. New pilot and process-development infrastructure comes online over the next three years.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="split">
  <div class="split-l">How we're funded</div>
  <div class="prose">
    <p>The BioHub launched with a <strong>$5.2 million investment from the Massachusetts Technology Collaborative</strong>, with cost-share from WPI, MBI, and corporate partners. WPI is also part of <strong>one of five NSF BioFoundries</strong> in the country, providing complementary federal support for strain-engineering infrastructure at the BioHub.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<p class="lead">The BioHub is governed by a <strong>Governing Board</strong> made up of WPI, MBI, MassTech, and stakeholders, supported by an <strong>Advisory Group</strong> providing strategic and technical guidance from across industry, academia, and government.</p>
